# Portuguese (`pt`)

Deinflection rules, text processors and scanning settings for Portuguese, in
the Brazilian and the European norm. Lookups start at the hovered word and try
only prefixes that end at a word boundary (`word` / `word` / `spaces`,
docs/languages.md 14.1). A hyphen separates words, and the whole hyphenated
text is also tried, so a verb with an attached pronoun reaches its infinitive
(`deixa-me` → deixar, `fazê-lo` → fazer, `dir-te-ei` → dizer), a hyphenated
compound reaches its own entry (`guarda-chuvas` → guarda-chuva), and the first
word of `franco-brasileira` is still looked up alone.

The recommended dictionaries, wty-pt-en and wty-pt-pt, give no part of speech:
wiktionary-to-yomitan fills the `rules` column only for the 16 languages of its
rule table (`src/dict/rules/valid.rs` at 4b02478), and Portuguese is not one:
all 572,340 rows of wty-pt-en and all 504,951 of wty-pt-pt leave it empty.
The pack therefore sets `lookup.untaggedTerms` to `"pass"` (docs/languages.md
4.4 and 9.4): an untagged term matches a deinflection whose
conditions include a dictionary form (`v`, `n`, `adj` or `adv`), and not one
that holds only the intermediate condition `vf` (Rule provenance). Without the field, no rule's output finds a
term of these dictionaries; only the dictionary's own form-of rows for the
text as written are followed.

wty-pt-en also has 32,556 phrase rows whose references carry a
negative-imperative rule: `não pode` → `["podar", ["third-person singular
imperative negative"]]`, since podar's present subjunctive is *pode*. A
hover on `não` matched such a phrase, the longer match, and showed its verb
(podar for *não pode vir*, fossar for *não fosse*). The pack lists the 20
negative-imperative rule names of wty-pt-en in `lookup.formOfIgnore`
(docs/languages.md 9.10), so these rows are no terms and `não` shows não
(Measurements 3).

## Rule provenance

`transforms.json` has 85 transforms and 1,420 rule rows (6,872 rules with
cross products expanded), every one `own`. Yomitan has no Portuguese rules, so
the own rules are also the union and the final set: `transforms.json` is
`sources/own.json` as `tools/merge-transforms.mjs pt` writes it ("1420 own, 0
yomitan, 0 both"). No Yomitan rule is left out, because there is none.

- **What the rules cover.** Tenses and persons are separate transforms, so a
  trace names both (`preterite (ele)`, `+lhe`):
  - the three conjugations in every simple tense and person: present,
    preterite (with the European `falámos`), imperfect, pluperfect, future,
    conditional, the present, imperfect and future subjunctive, the personal
    infinitive, the imperative of tu and vós, the gerund and the four
    participle forms. The imperative of você, nós and vocês is the present
    subjunctive and the regular tu imperative is the third person present, so
    those forms are deinflected once and traced as both (`present (ele) or
    imperative (tu)`, `present subjunctive (eu/ele) or imperative (você)`);
  - spelling changes: qu, gu and c before e (`fiquei`, `chegue`, `comece`);
    ç, j and g before a and o (`conheço`, `protejo`, `aja`, `distingo`); the
    gu of -guir and -guer kept before e (`segue`, `consegue`, `ergue`);
  - stem vowels: e → i and o → u in -ir verbs (`sinto`, `sirva`, `durmo`,
    `cubra`); u → o in a closed list of -ir verbs (`sobe`, `foge`, `consome`,
    `acode`, `cospe`); `agrido`, `previne`, `sigo`, `emirjo`; an accented í
    or ú after a vowel (`proíbe`, `reúne`, `saúda`); -ear verbs and -iar verbs
    conjugated like them (`passeio`, `odeio`, `anseia`, `negoceia`); -guar,
    -quar, -guir and -quir stressed on the stem (`enxáguo`, `averígua`,
    `delínquem`); -uir, -air, -oer and construir (`inclui`, `saímos`, `dói`,
    `constrói`); -uzir (`conduz`); and the accents the 1990 agreement removed
    (`enjôo`, `apóio`, `averigúe`);
  - irregular verbs, written out: ser, ir, estar, ter, haver, vir, ver, dar,
    fazer, dizer, trazer, poder, querer, requerer, saber, caber, pôr, ler,
    crer, rir, parir, prazer, reaver, perder, valer, ouvir, pedir, medir,
    prover, jazer, cear and gear, and the compounds of ter, vir, ver, ler, dar,
    fazer, dizer, pôr, querer, prazer, ouvir and pedir (`mantém`, `convém`,
    `desfez`, `compôs`, `impeça`), as cross-product rows that match only the
    listed prefixes; irregular participles (`escrito`, `aberto`, `ganho`,
    `pago`, `aceite`, `morto`, `preso`, `eleito`, `imerso`); the Brazilian
    colloquial `tô`, `tá`, `tamo`, `tava`;
  - pronouns attached to a verb, one transform per pronoun or contracted pair:
    enclisis (`deixa-me`, `disse-lhe`, `sentou-se`, `deu-mo`), `-lo` and `-la`
    after a dropped r, s or z (`fazê-lo`, `fá-lo`, `fê-lo`, `fazemo-lo`),
    `-no` and `-na` after a nasal (`fazem-no`, `dão-na`, `põe-nos`),
    `sentamo-nos`, two pronouns (`diz-se-lhe`), mesoclisis (`dir-te-ei`,
    `far-se-á`, `amá-lo-ei`), `ei-lo`, and the European `há-de` written before
    2009;
  - plurals (`casas`, `flores`, `leões`, `pães`, `homens`, `animais`,
    `papéis`, `fáceis`, `meses`, `países`, `hífenes`), loanwords and acronyms
    (`sites`, `ONGs`, `hobbies`), and compounds whose first word is plural
    (`segundas-feiras`); feminines (`bonita`, `trabalhadora`, `portuguesa`,
    `alemã`, `europeia`, `boa`, `atriz`, `avó`, `juíza`); diminutives in -inho
    and -zinho (`cafezinho`, `pãezinhos`, `pouquinho`, `rapazinho`, and names
    such as `Bentinho` → Bento); superlatives (`lindíssimo`, `felicíssimo`,
    `facílimo`, `paupérrimo`, `ótimo`, `rapidissimamente`, and the learned
    `fidelíssimo`, `antiquíssimo`, `simplicíssimo`, `nobilíssimo`); and -mente
    adverbs (`rapidamente`, `provavelmente`, `cortesmente`, `comumente`).
- **Conditions.** `v`, `n`, `adj` and `adv` (only for `eis`, the target of
  `ei-lo` and `eis-me`, which no rule takes further) are dictionary forms.
  `vf` is an intermediate state, a verb form whose attached pronoun was
  removed: only
  verb rules take it, so `chama-se` → `chama` (vf) → chamar (v) never shows
  the noun chama, and `deixa-me` never shows the noun deixa. A pronoun rule
  writes the infinitive directly where the form left is one (`dizer-lhe`,
  `fazê-lo`). Noun and adjective rules never take `vf`.
- **Ties between two lemmas.** wty-pt-en's form-of rows name both lemmas of
  a homograph (`sua`: seu and suar, to sweat; `soldados`: soldado and soldar,
  to weld), and without a part of speech both readings take one rule step and
  are confirmed by the word's rows, so they tie (docs/languages.md 9.6). A
  lookup then lists them in the order the rules find them: the longest
  matched ending first, then file order (7.4). At 04c95c7 that order put a
  rare homograph first for frequent words (first audit of #72: `sua` → suar,
  `pare` → parir, `amiga` → amigar, `achas` → acha). The rules now decide
  these ties as follows:
  - the transforms are declared in this order: plural, the verb forms (the
    present before the subjunctive), feminine, participle, then the rest.
    So `casas` → casa comes before casar, `pode` and `podem` → poder before
    podar, and `precisa` → precisar before preciso;
  - plurals are also written at the longer endings of verb forms, which would
    otherwise match first: -ados, -adas, -idos, -idas (`soldados`, `piadas`,
    `bebidas`), -eias and -iras (`ideias`, `mentiras`), -ques, -gues and
    -guas (`ataques`, `águas`), -ces (`alces`, not the subjunctive of
    alçar), -áveis (`saudáveis`, not the imperfect of saudar), -arias
    (`secretarias`, not the conditional of secretar), -ises (`crises`, not
    cris), and the irregular participles of the compounds of pôr, fazer, ver
    and dizer (`impostos`, `apostas`, `perfeitos`, `revistas`, `malditos`);
  - the feminine is written at the participle's endings -ada, -ida, -aída and
    -oída, so a feminine participle shows its adjective first (`cansada` →
    cansado, then cansar; `conhecida`, `distraída`). Irregular participles
    keep the verb first (`limpa` and `gasta` are also the present of limpar
    and gastar);
  - the plural and feminine at those endings put a noun first wherever
    wty-pt-en's -o or -a headword is a noun, which is right for `resultados`,
    `bebidas`, `estados` and `convidados` but not where that noun has another
    meaning and the text means the participle (second audit, MAJOR-3:
    `criada` as criado, a servant; `chamados`, `Forças Armadas`, `escolas
    privadas` as toilets). For the words of that class in the Tatoeba
    sentences and the texts of Measurements whose contexts point to the
    participle (ser, estar, ficar or an adverb before it, against a
    determiner or a preposition before the noun) or that the audit's verdicts
    in context found wrong, a whole-word rule gives the participle first, or
    the adjective where wty-pt-en has one (`privadas` → privado, `armadas` →
    armado, `quebradas` → quebrado);
  - the reverse tie, a verb form first where the text means a noun or an
    adjective (fourth audit, MAJOR-4, and fifth, MAJOR-5): a written-out
    irregular form (`dados` as dar's participle, `postos` as pôr's,
    `termos` as ter's personal infinitive, `vias` and
    `vinhas` as the tu imperfect of ver and vir), a longer verb ending
    (`baterias`, `garantias`, `saias`, `ruídos`), a short participle
    (`gastos`, `ganhos`, `rotas`, `surpresas`, `descobertas`) or the present
    before the feminine (`segura`, `completa`, `suja`, `ativa`, `larga`,
    `checa` in *República Checa*, `fixa`, `oculta`, `suma`, `ultima`). Where
    the Tatoeba sentences and the texts mean the noun or adjective (the
    audit's verdicts on every Tatoeba sentence of its 20 words, and samples
    read for the others), a whole-word rule gives it first; `caminha` gives
    caminhar before cama's diminutive;
  - since main's #83, a term that a `wholeWord` rule reaches ranks before one
    that only the word's rows confirm, and one that both reach ranks first
    of all (the standing key, docs/languages.md 9.6). The preferred readings
    below are such rules, so they keep their place; the superlative's

    `péssimas` and `péssimos` restore (sixth audit, m17);
  - the feminine plural of an adjective or determiner whose -a form is also

    reference of the word's rows, so `todas` showed toda (the Toda people)

  On the 459,646 words of the exclusions check below, these changes remove no
  deinflection and add 25 (the possessives, `tais`, `ladra`, `mestra`,
  `comumente` and the learned superlatives).
- **Stem constraints and exclusions.** Each has a test row that fails without
  it (Measurements, mutation checks):
  - every suffix rule's stem is the class `lowerStem`: no space and no capital
    letter. A rule therefore never reads the last word of a phrase as
    inflected (`te ama` is not a form of a phrase "te amar", `a vê-la` not of
    "a ver"), and a capitalised word is deinflected through its lowercase
    variant, so `Minha`, `Esta`, `Estamos` and `Porta` at the start of a
    sentence give meu, este, estar and porta, not the names Minho, Ester and
    Porto. Before the constraint these were 59 of the 71 wrong first results
    in *Dom Casmurro*, and `Estamos` → Ester alone came first at 930 places in
    Tatoeba while the constraint covered only the shortest endings;
  - a verb stem has at least two letters (`de`, `se` and `me` are not dar,
    ser and "mer"); an -ar ending in e never follows c or g, and an -er or -ir
    ending in a or o never follows c, g or a (`rege` is not regar, `vaga` not
    vagir, `peca` not pecer, and `sao`, São written without its accent, not
    sair, whose forms keep their i: saio);
  - `esta`, `estas`, `este`, `estes` and `esto` are not estar, whose forms are
    `está`, `estás`, `esteja`, `estou`; longer stems keep their readings
    (`estuda`, `estimas`, `estudo`);
  - exact stems whose regular-looking forms belong to another word. wty-pt-en
    has no part of speech to tell them apart, and after an attached pronoun no
    form-of row confirms the right lemma, so these readings came first
    (`Conte-me` → conter, `Serve-me` → server, `Afaste-se` → afar):
    - `conte`, `prove`, `rele`, `serve` are not conter, prover, reler or the
      noun server (those verbs have no regular present: contém, provê, relê),
      `prove` and `fale` not provir or the defective falir;
    - `queria`, `queres`, `querem` are querer, not the conjunction quer;
    - `servia`, `servido`, `servia-o` are servir, not the English noun server
      (the -er imperfect, the preterite's `-i` and the participle);
    - `afaste` is afastar, not a preterite of the noun afar;
    - `convida`, `intervia`, `provida`, `avia`, `sobrevia` are not vir
      compounds, whose imperfects and participles are irregular (convinha,
      convindo);
    - `alguem` (alguém without its accent, frequent in informal text) is not
      a subjunctive of algar, a dated noun, and `sida` (AIDS) not a feminine
      of sido, ser's invariable participle; `alegue` and `siderúrgica` keep
      alegar and siderúrgico.

    A literal constraint takes one string, so each rule is split by the
    excluded stems' first letters; a copy keeps the longer stems only where
    wty-pt-en has a word with one (`contorcer`, `convergir`, `querer`,
    `afastar`, `alegar`, `siderúrgico`). On 459,646 words (UniMorph por, both
    frequency lists and every one-word headword of wty-pt-en) these
    exclusions remove no deinflection a form-of row of the word names;
  - -guer is only erguer, and the plain -er present endings do not follow u:
    `aluga` and `alugue` are alugar, not the noun aluguer; the u → o verbs are
    a closed list, so `compre` and `corte` are not cumprir and curtir;
  - a loanword's plural -s follows only b, c, d, f, g, k, l, n, p, r, t, w or
    y (`miss`, `pass`, `mass` are not mis, pas, mas), and an acronym's only
    capitals (`ONGs`);
  - a diminutive name needs a capitalised stem of four letters (`Bentinho` →
    Bento; `Marinha`, the navy, is not Mara); -azinho is -az + inho
    (`rapazinhos` → rapaz, not rapa); -inho after l, r, s or z only
    (`papelinho`, `vozinha`; `gatinho` is not "gat");
  - the feminine -a → -o needs a stem of three letters (`duas` is not duo);
    -mente does not follow -a (`claramente` is claro, not clara); -uir does not
    follow g or q (`segui` is the preterite of seguir, not a present of
    "seguuir"); the -ir e → i change only applies between consonants (`seguia`
    is not "segueir").

## Known gaps

- **Spellings older than the 1911 and 1945 reforms** cause most wrong first
  results in real text: 341 of the 948 flagged in the 24 texts read as
  another word, and 5 of the 10 wrong in the hand-checked sample. Without a written accent
  the rules cannot tell which vowel carried it (`politicos` reads as a
  present of politicar, `sitios` of sitiar, `bôa` of "bôer"), and wty-pt-en
  writes many old spellings as references to a reference (`azas` → aza →
  asa), which one hop does not reach (docs/languages.md 16), so a regular
  verb reading comes first: `azas` → azar, `familias` → familiar,
  `deviamos` → devir. The old spellings the rules or the dictionary do reach
  keep their readings (`sahia` → sahir, `capellinha` → capella, `technicos`
  → técnico, `commercialmente` → comercial, and `peccados` → pecado through
  the plural at -ados and the row of peccado).
- **Modern forms written without their accent** (`viamos` for víamos,
  `saida` for saída, `ultima` for última) read as other words; the pack has
  no processor that adds accents.
- **The trace of an irregular short participle.** The participles of -ar
  verbs that are written whole (`ganha`, `gasta`, `paga`, `aceita`,
  `limpa`, `salva`) match before the present's -a, so a lookup shows the
  right verb with the trace "past participle" also where the present is
  meant (*Ela ganha pouco*); in Tatoeba about half the uses of each are the
  present. Likewise a word in -ares, -eres or -ires that is both a noun's
  plural and a verb form keeps one trace: the 34 of the plural row show
  "plural" also where the verb is meant (*para seres feliz*, *se andares
  depressa*, *para poderes entender*), and `falares`, `saberes`, `achares`,
  `comeres`, `quereres`, `acordares`, `viveres` and `cantares` the verb's
  trace also for the noun; a noun in -r outside the row, which occurs in
  none of the lists, Tatoeba or the texts, is traced as the personal
  infinitive. A feminine plural given its adjective by a whole-word reading
  is traced "feminine", where wty-pt-en's row says "plural feminine"
  (`todas`, `ótimas`, `privadas`). And `-nos` after a verb form in -m,
  -ão or -õe (`disseram-nos`, `dão-nos`, `põe-nos`) is traced "+os" (them):
  the rule for o and os after a nasal has the longer suffix, both paths take
  two steps, and the lookup keeps the one it found first. In Tatoeba, 19 of
  25 such hovers the eighth audit judged mean nos (us). The lemma is right.
- **Capitalised words at the start of a sentence** show a capitalised
  headword of wty-pt-en before the word itself when there is one, because
  the text as written needs no processor step and its decapitalised variant
  needs one (9.6): `As` shows the plural of the letter A before the article
  as, `O`, `A` and `E` the letters, `Como` the lake and `Este` the east,
  before the article, the conjunction or the demonstrative, and `Caminha`
  the town before caminhar. The word itself comes second. In Tatoeba this
  is `As` at 4,617 places and `O` at 36,329.
- **Own headwords of other words.** A word's own headword needs no rule, so
  it comes first (9.6), and for some frequent verb forms wty-pt-en's headword
  is another word: `era` shows the noun era (an age) before ser, `tinha`
  ringworm before ter, `estou` a telephone greeting before estar, `são`
  healthy before ser, `fala` speech before falar; `toda` (*toda a gente*)
  shows the Toda people before todo, `nova` news before novo, and `última`
  (*última vez*) recent news before último. The rules cannot change
  this; the verb or adjective comes second.
- **Two steps through a verb form's row.** With `"pass"`, a rule's output
  whose row has only form-of references is followed (9.10 item 2), so a
  derived word can reach a verb through a form it shares a spelling with:
  `solicitamente` gives solicito (solícito without its accent, which
  wty-pt-en lacks), whose row names solicitar; `beatificamente` →
  beatificar, `magnificos` → magnificar. In the fourteen texts 45 first
  results take the adverb's path, most of them related words
  (`regaladamente` → regalar).
- **Homographs the preferred readings do not cover** keep the order of Ties
  between two lemmas, which is right for most of them but not for all
  (Measurements 4 has the hand checks):
  - a participle whose -o or -a form is a noun of another meaning, where the
    participle is meant (`pisadas`, `jogadas`, `ouvidos` in *sermos
    ouvidos*), and the reverse for the words now given the participle
    (`criada` and `criados`, the maid and the servants of nineteenth-century
    novels; `passadas`, strides; `quebradas`, ravines): 20 of the second
    audit's 309 verdicts in the ten texts and 18 of its 111 in four more;
  - European tu forms whose noun is the more common reading in the Tatoeba
    sentences (`voltas`, `passas`, `passes`, `tocas`, `escutas`, `matas`),
    which show the noun, and the reverse for the tu forms given the verb:
    `metas` (goals, *metas fiscais*, BR #14,367), `falas` (*as suas falas*),
    `esperas`, `levas` (*as levas de imigrantes*) and `comas` (foliage) show
    meter, falar, esperar, levar and comer where the text means the noun,
    `vinhas` shows the noun vinha where vir's tu imperfect is meant (*O Primo
    Bazilio*), and
    `sais` (salts, *sais minerais*: 14 of its 19 Tatoeba sentences) shows
    sair's tu present, which European speech uses;
  - `chamadas` shows chamar, right for *são chamadas de* (48 of its 60

  - verbs and nouns the other way: `dura`, `murcha`, `distinta` and `gasta`

    bebê*), `termos` as ter's personal infinitive (*para termos*) and

    uses the fifth audit judged) now show the adjective and the noun, and *em termos de* shows wty-pt-en's
    phrase row for *termos de* (ter de), which matches more text;
  - in the novels of the nineteenth century, `criada` and `criados` are
    usually the maid and the servants, which the participle now hides: in
    *Os Maias* 53 of the 100 hovers whose first reading changed between
    e923f6f and b9e09f6 are these (third audit, m9); `queimadas` (forest
    fires) shows queimado (burnt), the reading of 6 of its 9 sentences;
  - the 35 forms of two verbs of one stem that show the rarer first
    (`unamos` → unar, `contemos` → conter, `surte` → surtir); feminine
    plurals of an adjective whose feminine is also a noun, beyond the 44
    readings: `fundas` → funda (a sling) before fundo; `baratas` and
    `técnicas`, which mean the adjective in about half their uses (*roupas
    baratas*, *escolas técnicas*), show the noun; `estéticas`,
    `narrativas` and `ruivas` show the noun where the sentences mean the
    adjective, and `claras` as egg whites, *as derivadas* and *saber as
    novas* the adjective where they mean the noun (5 of the 100 of the hand

    in -a that keep their verb (`precisa`, `acaba`, `dura`), and the
    reverse for those now given the adjective (*Larga a arma!*, 17 of the 52
    sentences of `larga`, shows largo);
    irregular participles shown as their verb (`isenta` → isentar), which the
    first audit counts as right; `vira` and `viras`, which show ver's
    pluperfect before virar; and `virei`, which shows vir before virar, as
    35 of the 50 Tatoeba sentences the seventh audit judged mean (14 mean
    virar).

  A host with a frequency dictionary ranks these ties by the chosen frequency
  before the order the rules found them in (9.6).
- **Names, foreign words and Roman numerals** are read as Portuguese words
  when they look like one: `Toni` → toner, `Elma` → elmo, `VII` → vir, the
  French `DES` → de, the Latin `quia` → quer, `Roqueirinha` → roqueira. A
  name's diminutive needs a stem of four letters, so `Aninha` shows aninhar,
  not Ana.
- **`gaps.tsv`** (37 rows the rules do not reach):
  - UniMorph rows that are not standard forms: `requiser`, `requisermos` and
    `requiseres` (requerer's preterite is regular: requeri), `reúnamos` and
    `reúnais` (no accent: reunamos), `soubéram`, `valeval`, `respigemos`
    (respiguemos);
  - lemmas in older or variant spellings: `agüentar` (from `aguentar`), `oppôr`,
    `decompôr`, `presupor`;
  - mobiliar stressed on the stem (`mobília`, `mobílies`), and `luz` (luzir),
    whose one-letter stem is shorter than the -uzir rule's minimum of two
    (`luz` is far more often the noun light);
  - wty-pt-en pairs that are not inflections: `padrastos` → "nadraste",
    `senhoras` → senhor@, `sobrinha` → sobrinhe and `otome` → otaku (gender
    terms), `cafetina` → cafetão;
  - diminutives and superlatives in other suffixes: -ito (`barquito`,
    `caracolito`), -ete and -eta (`diabrete`, `bosquete`, `roseta`), -ículo,
    -ilha, -zeco, -olas, and -inha forms that drop a vowel (`meinha` → meia,
    `historinha` → história, `saidinha` → saída, `bauzinho` → baú,
    `kitizinho` → kit); `mijoníssimo` (mijão), `acérrimo` (acre) and
    `péssimo` → ruim (the rules give mau).
- **Compounds whose words are both plural**, such as `amores-perfeitos`: the
  `replace` rule takes only the first `s-`, so the rules do not reach
  amor-perfeito; the word's form-of row does.
- **Not deinflected at all:** augmentatives (-ão, -ona, -aço: wty-pt-en has
  360 augmentative pairs, of which other rules reach 13), comparatives
  (melhor, pior, maior, menor, which wty-pt-en lists as headwords), and other
  derivations (-ito, -ete, -ura).
- **Exclusions remove readings no Portuguese form has**, by design: conter,
  prover, provir, reler and falir have no regular present, quer is not a verb,
  afar and server are nouns, and the vir compounds have irregular imperfects.
  `mear` is not written out (meio and meia are far more often half and sock),
  so its forms are not reached.
- **Without a part of speech** in the dictionary, the filter cannot drop a
  reading of the wrong kind: `fitos` (the adjective fito) shows fitar, and
  `remoque` (a noun) shows remocar. `untaggedTerms: "pass"` lets every rule's
  output match these untagged rows; `vf` keeps verb forms with a removed
  pronoun from matching nouns.
- **Runtimes.** A host must run hoshidicts at or after 487a6fc (#83) to load
  this pack: older engines refuse `lookup.formOfIgnore` (before #83) or
  `lookup.untaggedTerms` (before #58, 0a73e5e) as an unknown key.
  Hachidori's prebuilt WebAssembly at feat/multi-language 803f7b6e is built
  from b145feb, before it.
- **`traceSelection`** cannot be observed by a test (`lookup.tsv` compares a
  result's expression and matched text, not its trace), so it is set as the
  contract recommends (`shortest`).
