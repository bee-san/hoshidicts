# German (`de`)

Deinflection rules, text processors and scanning settings for German.
Lookups start at the hovered character and try every prefix of the scanned
text (`cursor` / `character` / `spaces`, docs/languages.md 14.1), so the
parts of a compound are found after the compound (`Hausaufgaben` gives
Hausaufgaben and Hausaufgabe, then Haus) and a separable verb is found with a particle at the
end of its clause (`kommt morgen an.` → ankommen).

## Rule provenance

`transforms.json` has 34 transforms and 231 rules, 4,922 with cross products
expanded: 28 transforms and 214 rules (4,624 expanded) `own`, one transform
and 5 rules (156) `both`, and 5 transforms and 12 rules (142) `yomitan`.
`sources/build-final.mjs` writes it: `sources/own.json` followed by five of
Yomitan's seven transforms. Yomitan's transforms come after the own ones, so
that where an own rule and a Yomitan rule reach different words at the same
cost, the own reading is found first and wins the tie (`gehört das?` →
gehören, not Yomitan's participle reading hören; `lookup.tsv`).

- **`both`**: an own rule that makes the same deinflection as a Yomitan rule.
  The own zu-infinitive rule has all 66 of the prefixes of Yomitan's 66
  zu-infinitive rules (`aufzuräumen` → aufräumen), and the four own
  participle rules of weak verbs make the deinflections of Yomitan's `ge…t`
  rules (`gescheitert` → scheitern, `dargestellt` → darstellen).
  `sources/yomitan-provenance.tsv` lists each of Yomitan's 144 rules, whether
  it is kept, and the own rules that make its deinflection.
- **Yomitan rules left out**: 132 of 144.
  - The 66 separated-prefix patterns (`^([L]+) .+ ab$` → `$1 ab`). They join
    a verb with the last word of any longer text that is a particle, also
    where the particle is a preposition or adverb or the start of a longer
    word (`sagte der Auf…` → aufsagen, `hast du da` → dahaben), and write the
    result as two words (`räum auf`), which only a dictionary's multi-word
    form-of rows read. On the four texts of the Measurements their readings
    are the first result at 6,429 positions judged wrong (1.13% of the
    Tatoeba lookups, 0.93% of *Der Prozess*), against 5 for the final
    rules; after an article, pronoun or auxiliary in Tatoeba
    at 1,478 (1.21%), against 0. The own rule needs the end of the clause
    after the particle and joins the two words; the final rules reach 145 of
    the sample's 150 separated forms (Known gaps has the other five).
  - The 66 zu-infinitive prefix rules (`anzu` → `an`). The own zu-infinitive
    rule makes all their deinflections. Because they need no ending, they also
    read `dazu ist` as `da ist` and `einzu…` as `ein…`: added to the final
    rules (`sources/variants.mjs`, `measure.mjs diff`), they change the first
    result at 12 positions of the four texts (`dazu ist` and `dazu war` → da
    sein, `einzuhängen`, `einzubohren` and `einzurammen` → einen,
    `heimzusteuern` → heimsen), all wrong, and give no reading the final
    rules lack.
- **Yomitan rules kept**: 12, in five transforms the own rules lack.
  `nominalization` (-ung → -en), `-bar`, `negative` (un-) and `-heit`
  (-heit, -keit) read derived words whole, and `past participle` (`ge…t` →
  -n, -en) also reads the archaic participles in -et after other consonants
  (`gestillet` → stillen, `cases.tsv`). Against the own rules alone they
  change the first result at 184 positions of the four texts
  (`sources/measure.mjs diff`): 175 better, nearly all a derived word read
  whole where the own rules read a part of it (`Beschämung` → beschämen,
  matched whole instead of `Beschäm`; `unmodern` → modern instead of the
  letter u; `Übersiedlung` → übersiedeln instead of übers), 4 worse
  (`unbeeinflußt`, whose own form-of row was first, gives beeinflusst;
  `unheilverkündenden` gives heil instead of Unheil; `Unehrenhaftes` and
  `unehrenhaften` give ehrenhaft instead of Unehre) and 5 wrong either way
  (`Unum` twice, `Ungeschick`, `unabweislich`, `entschuldbar`). A reading through
  `negative` names it in its trace (`unmöglich` → möglich [negative]), as in
  Yomitan.
- **Own rules**: each tense and person is a transform of its own, so a trace
  names both (`past (wir, sie)`, `present (du)`, `subjunctive II (ich, er)`):
  - the regular endings of weak verbs, with the -e- after d, t, m and n
    (arbeitest, atmet) and the stems in -el and -er (sammle, wandere), in the
    present, past, subjunctive I and imperative;
  - a table of 161 strong verbs and 17 mixed and modal verbs, with sein, haben,
    werden and tun: the present forms with a vowel change (gibt, fährt, liest),
    the imperative (gib, nimm, sieh), the past and subjunctive II stems (ging,
    käme, brächte) with their endings, and the participle. The forms of the
    table also read the verbs with a prefix (verstand, bekam, aufstand), and
    their spelling before 1996 (schloß, mußte, läßt, gewußt);
  - participles: ge- inside a separable verb (eingekauft, angefangen,
    kennengelernt), a strong participle after an inseparable prefix without
    ge- (verstanden, bekommen, missverstanden, anerkannt), present
    participles (lächelnd), and both declined (gekochten, lächelnde);
  - the zu-infinitive of separable verbs (anzurufen), with 152 particles;
  - a separable particle at the end of a clause, joined back to its verb
    (`kommt morgen an.` → ankommt → ankommen, `Hör auf!` → aufhören);
  - noun plurals in -e, -er, -n, -en, -s, -nen (Lehrerinnen) and -se
    (Ergebnisse, Busse), with umlaut (Bäume, Häuser, Mütter, Äpfel, Gärten),
    Latin, Greek and Italian plurals (Museen, Themen, Rhythmen, Risiken,
    Materialien), the dative plural (Kindern) and the genitive (des Vaters,
    des Hauses, des Namens, Hans');
  - adjective endings, also of adjectives in -el, -er and -e (dunkle, teure,
    leisen) and the dropped e of older texts (höhern, andern), hohe for hoch,
    comparatives and superlatives with and without umlaut (älter, größte,
    besser, meiste, nächste);
  - contractions of a preposition and the article (zum, im, ins, beim, aufs)
    and a verb with `'s` (gibt's, geht's).
- **Processors**: `decapitalize`, `capitalizeFirstLetter` and
  `eszettPreprocessor` (ß → ss and ss → ß, `daß` → dass, `Strasse` → Straße)
  are Yomitan's; `normalizeUnicode` (NFKC), `removeSoftHyphens`,
  `normalizeApostrophes` (’ → ', `geht’s` → geht's) and `capitalizeWord` are
  the pack's own. None is optional, so the pack has no settings.
- **Conditions**: Yomitan's `v` (with `vw` and `vst`), `n` and `adj`, which
  are the part-of-speech tags of wiktionary-to-yomitan's German dictionary,
  and two of the pack's own that no dictionary row has: `sep` (a finite verb
  whose separated particle was put back in front of it) and `np` (a plural
  whose dative -n was removed).
  - The separated-prefix rule gives only `sep`, which only the rules for
    finite forms read, so a word before the particle that is not a finite
    verb gives nothing (`lächelnd an.` is not anlächeln, `lookup.tsv`). The
    first and third person plural, written like the infinitive, are read by
    the `present (wir, sie)` rules (`fangen wir an?` → anfangen).
- **Capital letters**: German writes nouns with a capital letter.
  - Verb and adjective endings are removed only from a word in small
    letters, so a capitalized noun is not read as a verb (`Kunde` is not
    kunden, `cases.tsv`).
  - The noun rules read the word in small letters and write the capital
    letter back (`then`, a `map` step), so they read a noun through
    `decapitalize`, at one processor step. A word at the start of a sentence
    keeps its own reading in small letters first, because a lookup ranks
    fewer processor steps first: `Sagen Sie …` is sagen, not the plural of
    Sage, and `Alles` is alles, not the genitive of All (`lookup.tsv`). The
    With noun rules that read capitalized words, as the first version's did,
    a noun reading was the first result for 513 of the 5,954 frequent words
    written in small letters when they start a sentence (3.30% of the list's
    tokens; `measure.mjs start`); now 19 (0.02%), nearly all English names
    of the subtitle list (Eddie → Eddi).
  - `capitalizeWord`, a processor of this pack, reads a word written in
    capitals as a noun at one step (`FRAU` → Frau, `KÜNDIGUNG` → Kündigung).
    On the 224 words in capitals of the four texts it changes the first
    result at 15 (`measure.mjs capitals`): 10 better (Verhaftung,
    Untersuchung, Maler, Ende), 2 worse (`DES` → the note Des), 3 neither.
- **Stem constraints**: every regular ending needs a stem of letters, digits
  and hyphens, never a space, so it does not fire on the last word of a
  longer text (`das macht` is not `das machen`), and every regular verb and
  adjective ending a stem of two letters (`je` is not jen). The listed forms
  of the verb table have no such constraint: they also read the verb of a
  multi-word dictionary entry (`zu Abend gegessen` → zu Abend essen, `recht
  hat` → Recht haben, `in Anspruch genommen` → in Anspruch nehmen), and on a
  phrase that is no entry they give nothing a lookup shows (`er ging` → `er
  gehen`). Such idiom readings are a first result at about 320 of the
  616,124 lookups of the Measurements, mostly right; a few are wrong (`Auto
  fuhr gegen …` → Auto fahren, Known gaps). Other constraints, each with a
  row of `cases.tsv` or `lookup.tsv` that fails without it
  (`sources/mutations.mjs`):
  - du -st and the superlative -st not after s, ß, x or z (`weisst` is not
    weisen); -est and -et only after d, t, m or n, where the e belongs to the
    present (`machest` and `machet` are the subjunctive I); the genitive and
    plural -s not after s, ß, x or z (`ass` is not As);
  - the bare imperative not after a vowel, d or t (`frei` is not freien), nor
    for `schloß`, the spelling before 1996 of schloss; the present (ich) -e
    not for the stem mein (`meine` is the possessive mein, which the
    dictionary reaches through its form-of row, not meinen; the row still
    names meinen);
  - the listed verb forms not after c, since no prefix ends in c (`schießt`
    is not sc + hießt → scheißen, `schiebt` not schauen); no short
    subjunctive II form that is the present of another verb (`führt` is not
    fahren: ten stems found in wty-de-en, listed in `build-own.mjs`); the
    listed adjective forms only as one word (`sehr best…`, the start of `sehr
    bestimmt`, is not sehr gut);
  - plural -n and the dative plural only after e, l or r (`Lohn` is not Loh,
    `Mann` not Man); plural -s only after a vowel and -s for consonants not
    after s, ß, x, z; the plain -er plural not after k (`Techniker` is not a
    plural of Technik); the umlaut plural only on the last vowel, before
    consonants (`Mädchenhaare` is not Madchenhaar); the Latin -en for -o only
    after d, k or t (`Lassen` is not Lasso), for -is only after s or x
    (`Jeden` is not Jedis), -ien only after l, p or z (`Photographien` is
    not Photograph);
  - the separated particle never reads an article as the verb (`der Hand
    hin.` is not hindern), and needs a punctuation mark or und or oder after
    it (`wartet auf den Bus` is not aufwarten).
- **Homographs**: a word that is a form of two headwords at the same cost
  (matched length, processor steps, trace length) shows first the one the
  lookup finds first (docs/languages.md 9.6): the longest matched ending
  first, and at one ending the earlier transform. wty-de-en usually names
  both readings in the word's own form-of row, so both are
  dictionary-confirmed and the order decides. The pack orders them so that
  the reading a word nearly always has comes first, each choice with a row
  of `lookup.tsv` that fails without it:
  - the adjective transforms come first, before the verb and noun
    transforms: `schöne` is schön, not schönen; `weiße`, `gleiche`,
    `dunkle`, `einige` (einig) likewise. Of 21 frequent words that are an
    adjective form and a 1sg present, `sources/context-counts.py` finds the
    adjective use more often for 14 (`schöne` 125 to 8) and the verb for 7
    (`liebe` 125 to 113, `lasse`, `heiße`, `wette`, `schicke`, `leide`,
    `bereite`): the present (ich) of those 7 is listed, a longer ending, so
    it is found first (`liebe dich` → lieben);
  - endings that keep the stem's last letters, so that the adjective reading
    matches as many letters as the verb's: -ten → -te (`letzten` is letzte,
    not the obsolete letzen + -ten), -ere → -er (`bessere`, `sichere`: not
    bessern, sichern), and an adjective in -e writes its ending with that e
    (`bösen` is böse, not the plural of Böse, `leisen` leise);
  - adjectives in -t whose declined forms are spelled like the past of a
    rare verb are listed: bekannt, berühmt, bestimmt, bewaffnet, doppelt,
    kompliziert, recht, verdammt, verehrt, verfickt, verflucht, vereinigt,
    verrückt (`verrückte` is verrückt, not verrücken + -te). For participles
    of common verbs the past is the more frequent use (12 such words,
    `erklärte`, `versteckte`, `passierte` …: 121 verb uses against 17
    adjective uses, `context-counts.py`), and the verb comes first;
  - the present (er, ihr) of a verb whose stem ends in t is written with the
    t (`kostet`: kosten + -et, not kosen + -tet), and schalten's present is
    listed before schelten's past (`schaltet`);
  - participles that are spelled like the present of a verb in ge- are
    listed (`gewartet` is warten, not the archaic gewarten; `gelobt`,
    `geleitet`, `angehört`, `zugehört`), and schalten's imperative `schalt`
    before schelten's past;
  - the plural -n of a noun in -e is written with the e (`Stunden` is
    Stunde, not Stund; `Katzen`, `Reisen`, `Akten`, `Kohlen`, `Tabletten`
    likewise).

  The ties the pack cannot order are in Known gaps.
- **History**: the first commit of this pack, `de: own rules` (711bd21), has
  the own rules as written before Yomitan's file was read (29 transforms, 231
  rules, 4,444 with cross products expanded). Later commits changed them:
  - with the port of Yomitan's rules, the conditions both files have were
    given Yomitan's definitions (`v` with the subconditions `vw` and `vst`,
    `n` without subconditions, which the merge tool requires; the own `ns`
    was dropped and `np` is no longer a dictionary form), and the own ß
    processors (one of them optional, with a setting for Swiss spelling)
    gave way to Yomitan's `eszettPreprocessor`, which reads ß as ss and ss
    as ß;
  - after the measurements below: the noun rules read words in small letters
    and write the capital letter back, the separated-prefix rule gives only
    finite verbs, and the constraints above, each where a measurement found
    a wrong first result; the -er, -el and -en umlaut plurals no longer
    change äu, which no noun does; the old spellings of the listed verb
    forms, haben's participle, the -ien verbs (knien), the -ern and -erm
    adjective endings, more particles (abhanden, beiseite, schwarz, stehen
    …), and `Busse`, `Säle` and `Neubauten`;
  - after the first audit (audit-hachi-lang-de, MAJOR 1): the order of
    homographs above (the adjective transforms had come after the verb
    transforms and before the nouns), `meine`, and the rule `nisse` → `nis`,
    which `isse` → `is` covers, removed.

## Measurements

Every number is with wty-de-en as its release publishes it, form-of rows
included, imported by `hoshidicts-cli import`, at hoshidicts 1fed676 with
this pack (GCC 14.3, Release). Items 2 to 4 were measured at 6a65eac and
again after the rebase on 1fed676 (#58 adds `lookup.untaggedTerms`), with
identical output. The union is `tools/merge-transforms.mjs de --out`, which
puts Yomitan's transforms first. The pack has no optional
processor, transform or variant pass, so every number is the same with every
option on and with the defaults; a copy of `cases.tsv` with the line
`# options: default` added gives the same numbers as the file.

## Known gaps

- **The 36 UniMorph rows of `gaps.tsv`**:
  - 20 archaic, obsolete or regional strong forms the verb table leaves out:
    `muhl` and `muhlt` (mahlen), `flisse`, `flisst` and `beflisst`
    (fleißen, befleißen), `kort` and `erkorst` (küren, erküren), `schrak`
    and `schraken` (schrecken), `splisst`, `schloff`, `erblichst`, `erlosch`
    (erleschen), `vermaß` (vermeßen), `bisse` (beyßen), `gerieth`, `räth`,
    `riehst`, `empfäht`, `frägst`;
  - 10 forms or lemmas UniMorph spells otherwise than German does
    (`Millardenmarkt`, `Bestimmungswort` for Bestimmungwörter, `ettapiere`,
    `ermunteretet`, `spitzst`, `demoliertst`, `umwarbtest`, `krankesten`,
    `schwuligem` → schwulstig, `Sternensysteme` → Sternsystem);
  - 2 Swiss lemmas (`abfliessen`, `erschiessen`), 3 particles the pack does
    not list (`stufst höher.`, `stapeltest tief.`, `loadetet up.`) and `Asse`
    → As, whose stem has one letter.
- **Particle verbs from the dictionary's rows**: wty-de-en has form-of rows
  for the separated forms of its separable verbs (`ging zu` → zugehen, `hat
  da` → dahaben; 43,447 rows of a verb form and a separable particle, tagged
  v, that name a verb starting with the particle, `measure.mjs separated`;
  43,853 without the v tag). A lookup reads the longest text it finds, so where
  the verb is followed by a preposition or adverb with the same spelling, the
  particle verb comes first: 3,711 first results in the four texts (0.60% of
  the lookups, 0.40-0.66% per text), with every rule set (0.34-0.55% with
  Yomitan's rules). No rule takes part.
- **Capitalized entries at the start of a sentence**: for 871 of the 5,954
  frequent words written in small letters (23.53% of the list's tokens), the
  first entry at the start of a sentence is the capitalized word's own row
  (Ich, Es, Haben) or form-of row (`Danke` → Dank), with every rule set. Such
  a row needs no processor step, and a lookup does not know where a sentence
  starts.
- **Separated particles beyond the scan**: the particle is joined only within
  the scan length a host passes (16 code points by default, as in Yomitan).
  Of the clauses in the four texts that end with the particle of a separated
  form the dictionary lists, 1,304 of 5,679 (23%) fit in 16 code points
  (`measure.mjs separated`).
  With a scan length of 40, the right several-word readings of *Der Prozess*
  rise from 1,654 to 1,917, the particle verbs of the dictionary's rows fall
  from 381 to 355, and the rule's wrong readings rise from 0 to 2.
- **Homographs the pack cannot order** (docs/languages.md 9.6, a framework
  issue hachi-lang-lead accepted for a later framework pull request on
  2026-11-03): when both readings of a word are dictionary-confirmed and tie
  on matched length, steps and trace length, the one the lookup finds first
  comes first. The rules decide that order (Rule provenance, Homographs)
  except here:
  - noun readings of a lowercase word whose noun is confirmed through the
    capitalized spelling's own row (`zehnten` → Zehnt, `zweier` and `dreier`
    → Zwei and Drei, `oberste` → Oberst, `grade` → Grad, `seins` → Sein,
    `draus` → Drau, `grosse` → Gros; 34 first results in Tatoeba and 18 in
    the books, and 6 of the 8 wrong frequent words of Measurements 2). The
    planned `lookup.confirmedOrder: "references"` orders such ties by the
    matched text's own references, which name the adjective, numeral or
    adverb;
  - `heut` → heuen (to make hay) before heute: the rules reach heuen and its
    row is confirmed; excluding the stem would also lose `heult` (heulen).
- **Homographs of common verbs and adjectives**: where the pack orders a
  homograph by the more frequent reading, the other reading is second:
  `liebe Mama` and `das heiße Bad` show lieben and heißen, and participle
  adjectives after a determiner show the past of their verb (`die entfernten
  Dinge` → entfernen, `den Besiegten`); 9 of the 12 wrong positions of the
  rule-changed sample (Measurements 4). `gehört` shows gehören before hören
  (in Tatoeba 124 of its 236 occurrences follow an auxiliary, where it is
  hören's), and `Zwecken` shows Zwecke (tacks) before Zweck, since the plural
  -en of nouns in -e comes first.
- **Names**: the plural and genitive rules read names the dictionary lacks
  (`Eddie` → Eddi, `Janes` → Jan, `Angeles` → Angel), mostly English names
  in subtitles and Tatoeba.
- **Idioms through the listed verb forms**: `Auto fuhr gegen …` → the idiom
  Auto fahren, `… haben könnte.` → haben können (Rule provenance, Stem
  constraints).
- **The dictionary's abbreviations and phrases** win by length: `so.` →
  So. (Sunday) and `komm.` → Komm. at the end of a sentence, `z.` → Z.,
  `alter Mann` → the mining term Alter Mann, `ein Paar` → ein paar, and the
  scan cutting a word (`sei Dank` → da sein, `hat Liebeskummer` → lieb
  haben): 9 of the 60 hand-judged positions.
- **Words in capitals**: `capitalizeWord` reads them as a noun at the cost
  of the small-letter word, so a function word in capitals with a
  capitalized homograph gets it (`DES` → Des and `ES` → Es, the notes):
  2 of the 224 words in capitals of the four texts.
- **Separated particles that belong elsewhere**: `ab und zu` (now and then)
  is read as the particle ab (`komm mich ab und zu mal besuchen` →
  abkommen), a zu-infinitive before the particle of another verb as its verb
  (in `fing gleich zu schreiben an.`, `schreiben` gives anschreiben), and a
  noun spelled like a verb form as the verb (`Schoß ein,` → einschießen).
- **Traces of forms that are also other forms**: the comparative `schöner`
  is shown as declined, the dative singular in -e (`dem Hause`) as a plural,
  and participles without ge- (`verkauft`, `studiert`) as the present (er,
  ihr); 136 of the 1,764 sample rows the final rules reach.
- **Yomitan's derivations**: `negative` reads un- adjectives as their base
  with a `negative` step (`unehrenhaft` → ehrenhaft) and `-bar` can reach
  another verb (`entschuldbar` → entschulden, not entschuldigen).
- **Not reached**: reflexive verbs from a pronoun (`freut sich` gives
  freuen; wty-de-en has 47 headwords that start with sich), `Säue` → Sau
  (an umlaut at the end of the stem), and strong verbs outside the table
  except through their regular endings.
