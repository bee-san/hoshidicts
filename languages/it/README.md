# Italian (`it`)

Deinflection rules, text processors and scanning settings for Italian.
Lookups start at the hovered word and try only prefixes that end at a word
boundary (`word` / `word` / `spaces`, docs/languages.md 14.1), so a pronoun
and its verb are read together (`mi lavo` → `lavarsi`, `se ne va` →
`andarsene`, `ce la faccio` → `farcela`), an elided article or preposition
is read with its word (`l'amico` → `amico`, `dell'acqua` → `acqua`), and a
word longer than the scan length is still looked up whole.

**The recommended dictionary has no part-of-speech tags, so the pack sets
`lookup.untaggedTerms: "pass"`.** wiktionary-to-yomitan writes no `rules`
column for Italian: wty-it-en and wty-it-it leave it empty on every row. With
`"pass"` (sections 7.2 and 9.4) an untagged row carries D, every condition
with `isDictionaryForm: true` (`v n adj adv`), so the rules' readings reach
it. With `"filter"`, the default and Yomitan's behaviour, a deinflection with
conditions matches no term of such a dictionary, and only the text as
written, its processor variants and the dictionary's own form-of rows find
terms. Every measurement below uses wty-it-en as published with the pack as
committed, except in Hachidori, whose engines predate the setting
(Measurements 5); `eval-language`, the frequent forms and the real text are
also given for a copy of the pack set to `"filter"`.

## Rule provenance

`transforms.json` has 96 transforms and 6,863 rules as the engine counts them
(`language-conformance-test`), in 974 rule objects: `sources/build-own.mjs`
writes the suffix rules of a transform that share their conditions and
constraints as one circumfix in cross-product form (docs/languages.md 7.3).
Every transform and rule is `own`; there is no `yomitan` or `both` rule.

- **Yomitan's rules**: none. Yomitan d3c54c1 has no Italian deinflection
  rules (`ext/js/language/it/` holds only `italian-processors.js`, and
  `tools/port-yomitan-transforms.mjs` reports "Yomitan has no
  ext/js/language/it/*-transforms.js"). So there is no `sources/yomitan.json`,
  the union is the own set (`node tools/merge-transforms.mjs it` writes
  `transforms.json` byte for byte), and no Yomitan rule is left out.
- **Yomitan's processors**: all four are in `language.json` under their ids
  and `tools/check-yomitan.mjs` compares them. Two are narrowed, and
  `sources/yomitan-differences.tsv` lists both:
  - `removeAlphabeticDiacritics` strips accents only from a lower-case single
    word. Yomitan's also strips them from a capitalised word, which then
    reaches a proper name before the word itself (`Già` → Gia, `Sarà` →
    Sara, `Tornò` → Torno), and from phrases (`è poi` → `e poi` → eppoi).
    With Yomitan's processor, 164 instead of 98 of the 233,208 Gutenberg
    lookups below have a first result the dictionary does not confirm
    (`sources/measure.py text` on a copy with it).
  - `capitalizeFirstLetter` turns a final capital I back into i, so the word
    `i` gets no capitalised variant. wty-it-en has no entry for the article
    i, only form-of rows (il, la, lo, gli, le, l' and the letter I). The
    variant `I` found the letter during the scan, and ties go to the
    candidate found first (9.6), so `i` showed the letter I; it now shows il
    (a whole-word plural rule). `i` is 1,281,797 of it_50k's tokens. The cost
    is a useless variant for words that end in a capital I (`XVI` → `XVi`),
    and `VI.` as a chapter number shows the pronoun `Vi` instead of `vi` (6
    Gutenberg lookups).
- **Own rules** (`sources/build-own.mjs`): one transform per tense or mood and
  person, so a trace names both (`imperfect (noi)`, `past historic (lui,
  lei)`).
  - the regular endings of -are, -ere, -ire and -isc- verbs in every simple
    tense and mood, the gerund and both participles;
  - spelling: -care/-gare (`cerchi`, `pagherei`), -ciare/-giare
    (`comincerò`), -iare (`studi`, `studino`), -gliere (`tolgo`, `togli`),
    -gnere (`spengo`), -acere (`piaccio`, `tacque`), -ucire (`cucio`), -cere
    participles in -ciuto;
  - irregular verbs, with their compounds: essere, avere, the modals, sapere,
    andare, dare, stare, fare, dire, bere, venire, tenere, uscire, salire,
    rimanere, valere, parere, apparire, comparire, dolere, morire, udire,
    sedere, nuocere, cuocere, muovere (and the unstressed o of muovere,
    cuocere, nuocere, scuotere), porre, trarre, -durre, compiere and the
    contracted futures (vedrò, vivrò, cadrò, godrò);
  - the strong past historic and past participle of about 120 verbs (`presi`,
    `scrissi`, `chiuso`, `rimasto`), which their compounds share
    (`compresi`, `sorpreso`);
  - enclitic pronouns, one transform per pronoun or pair (41): a clitic rule
    removes them after an infinitive without its final e, a gerund or an
    imperative (`farlo`, `dicendomi`, `guardalo`; `dammi`, `dillo`, `vattene`
    for the monosyllabic imperatives), and the `infinitive` transform reads
    the infinitive (`far` → `fare`, `por` → `porre`);
  - pronominal verbs. A pronoun written before a verb is marked
    (`proclitic`: `mi lavo` → `mi+lavo`), the verb rules read the marked verb
    and give only its person, and the `pronominal` rule gives the -rsi verb
    when the pronoun agrees with that person: `mi lavo`, `ti lavi`, `si
    lava`, `ci laviamo`, `vi lavate` give `lavarsi`, while `mi lava` (it
    washes me) does not; `me ne vado` gives `andarsene`, `me la cavo`
    `cavarsela`, `ce la faccio` `farcela`, `c'entra` `entrarci`, `ci penso`
    `pensarci`. A reflexive pronoun attached to an imperative agrees with its
    person (`alzati`, `alziamoci`, `alzatevi`; `alzami` is only alzare). The
    negative imperative `non ti preoccupare` gives `preoccuparsi`. `c'è`,
    `ci sono`, `ci vuole`, `ce ne vuole`, `ce l'ho` and `ce l'ho fatta` are
    written out;
  - clitic climbing (`climbing`): a pronoun before stare or a modal that
    agrees with it belongs to the gerund or infinitive after it (`mi sto
    preparando` → `prepararsi`, `si sta divertendo` → `divertirsi`, `mi devo
    alzare` → `alzarsi`, `me ne voglio andare` → `andarsene`); an object
    pronoun (`mi sta guardando`, `mi devi aiutare`) gives no -rsi verb;
  - the past participle of a pronominal verb (`pentito` → `pentirsi`,
    `inginocchiata` → `inginocchiarsi`), read through a mark only the
    participle rules write, so an infinitive as written never gives the -rsi
    verb;
  - plurals (with the hard and soft c and g, -cia/-gia, -io, -ati read as
    -ato before the enclitic -ti, loanwords in -s, and the irregular
    `uomini`, `dei`, `buoi`, `uova`, `braccia`, `dita`, `orecchie`, the
    article `i` …), feminines (adjectives in -a; nouns for
    people in -trice, -essa, -ona, -iera, -ora), superlatives in -issimo and
    adverbs in -mente.
- **Ties and preferred readings.** When a word's rows name two lemmas that
  the rules reach with traces of one length, the lookup shows first the one
  it found first (docs/languages.md 9.6): the reading through the longer
  ending, and for one ending, the transform and rule that come first in
  `transforms.json`. Four choices of order decide most such ties. Each was
  measured on the 10,000 most frequent words of it_50k with every preferred
  reading removed, and the words whose first result it decides were judged
  one by one (the evidence files `order-diff-judged.tsv`,
  `fp-diff-judged.tsv`, `o-diff-judged.tsv` and `ati-diff-judged.tsv`):
  - the noun and adjective transforms come before the verb slots: `figli`
    is figlio before figliare, `chiavi` chiave before chiavare. Of the 337
    words this order decides, the noun order is right for 227 and the verb
    order for 47; 46 are used both ways and keep the noun (`ricordi`,
    `lavori`, `leggi`, `aspetti`, `scherzi`), and for 17 neither is right
    (`cosi`, così without its accent; `armi`, `conti`, `regali`, `temi`);
  - the plural comes before the feminine plural: of the 55 words this
    decides, the plural's noun is right for 23 (`volte`, `idee`, `porte`,
    `amiche`) and the feminine plural's adjective for 21 (`nuove`,
    `piccole`, `prime`, `vecchie`, whose feminine singular is also a rarer
    noun: nuova "news", piccola "a small beer"); 7 are used both ways and 4
    are neither;
  - for -i, -e comes before -o: of 21 words, 12 need the -e noun (`mesi`,
    `parti`, `cani`, `tali`) and 8 the -o one (`morti`, `soli`, `conti`,
    `lenti`); `arti` is used both ways;
  - -ati is also read as the plural of -ato through an ending longer than
    the enclitic -ti, so the participle or adjective comes before the
    reflexive imperative where the word's rows name both (`condannati`,
    `informati`, `destinati`). This changes 4 of the top 10,000 (three used
    both ways, one neither) and 80 words of ranks 10,001-50,000: 71 better,
    2 worse (`sfogati`, `affrettati`), 6 used both ways and 1 neither.

  For the words these orders still get wrong, `PREFERRED` in
  `sources/build-own.mjs` has 97 whole-word rules. A whole-word rule is
  found before any ending, so it reaches the reading the bare word nearly
  always has and leaves the other second. They are the 47 verbs of the first
  list (`serve`, `credi`, `continua`, `ami`, `pare`, `torni`, and the
  imperatives `alzati`, `fidati`, `figurati`), the 21 feminine plural
  adjectives, the 8 -o plurals, `armi`, `vari`, `temi` (arma, vario, tema)
  and `sali` (salire), and 17 ties the frequency proxy of Measurements 2
  found between two other readings (`rischi` → rischio, not risco; `omicidi`
  → omicidio; `stronzate` → stronzata, not stronzare; `private` → privato;
  `domando` → domandare, not domare's gerund; `riposi` → riposare, not
  riporre), with `comperò` and `comperai` (comprare), found in a text. Each
  was chosen by hand from these lists, so the top 10,000 is in-sample for
  them; ranks 10,001-50,000 are held out (Measurements 2). A frequency
  dictionary would order such ties too, since the lookup compares
  frequencies before the order of discovery.
- **Stem constraints and exclusions.** Each is there for a measured wrong
  reading, and each has a test that fails without it (`sources/mutate.py`,
  Measurements 7):
  - regular endings need a verb stem of two letters (`da`, `di` and `mare` are
    not dare, dire and mandare); fare, dare, dire and bere are written out;
  - every affix rule reads a stem of lower-case letters, one word: a
    capitalised word is read through its decapitalised variant, so `Ieri` is
    not the surname Iero and `Rido` not the name Ridere, and no rule turns the
    last word of a phrase into another phrase (`a casa` is not a caso, `non
    mi disse` not the idiom non mi dire, `un'ora sola` not ora solare);
  - plurals and feminines need a stem of two letters (`si` is not so, `me`
    not ma, `la` not lo);
  - the plain -are endings in e and i leave out stems in c and g (`baci` is
    baciare, not bacare); the past historic in -etti, -ette, -ettero needs a
    stem in d, m, t or v (`casette` is not casere);
  - `dove`, `sede`, `sala`, `vena` and `mora` are not forms of dovere,
    sedere, salire, venire and morire, whose forms are deve, siede, salga,
    venga and muoia;
  - dare and stare are read only as words and with the prefixes of their
    compounds (`credette` is not cre + dette), and stessi and stesse are not
    stare's (they are stesso's plurals; wty-it-en's rows still name stare);
    fare's compounds are not read after a stem in f (`affaccia`, `affatto`,
    in the participles too; contraffare and sopraffare are written out);
    porre's compounds need a prefix of two letters (`sposi`, `sposto`);
    dire's past historic is read only alone and with its compounds'
    prefixes (`disse`, `predisse`, `benedisse`), since -disse also ends the
    imperfect subjunctive of -dire verbs (`impedisse`, `tradisse`);
  - after a pronoun the verb's own stem has three letters and volere and ridare
    are left out (`si scava` is not scarsi, `si vola` not volersi, `ti ridi`
    not ridarsi), stare gives no starsi, a lone si before a modal is
    impersonal (`si può fare` is not farsi), and the -arre verbs are trarre's
    compounds, written out (`si distrae` → distrarsi; `si mostrasse` is not
    read through trarre's -trasse);
  - a pronoun before the present subjunctive singular agrees only with lui
    and Lei (`si accomodi`, `che si lavi`): mi and ti before it are objects
    far more often (`mi dica`, `ti piaccia`, `ti dia`), so they give no -rsi
    verb; tu's pairs te ne and te la still do (`che te ne vada` →
    andarsene);
  - `ce la` before a verb of any person is read as the -cela verb before the
    reflexive pairs (`ce la faremo` is farcela, not farsela), while `ce ne`
    keeps the pairs first (`ce ne andiamo` is andarsene); `ci siamo` is left
    to the dictionary, whose row names essersi (it is mostly the reflexive
    compound past, `ci siamo sposati`);
  - superlatives of dolce and triste are not dolco and tristo; `comperato` is
    not comperarsi.
- **History.** The first commit, "it: own rules", has the own rules as
  written before Yomitan's file was looked at: 93 transforms and 3,825 rules
  as the engine counts them. Later commits changed them after measuring
  with wty-it-en, Tatoeba and the Gutenberg texts, and each commit message
  says why: the proclitic mark and the person conditions, so that pronominal
  readings need the verb's person; the enclitic strip followed by the
  `infinitive` transform; the one-word and lower-case stems; the orthographic
  and exact-stem exclusions; the negative imperative; clitic climbing; the
  participle mark; the narrowed `removeAlphabeticDiacritics`; and
  `addElisionApostrophe` for lower-case words only (initials such as `V.` are
  not v'). Two audits (reports/audit-hachi-lang-it.md, NOT_OK at 3b40ab0,
  and audit-hachi-lang-it-b.md, NOT_OK at 7a1ed92, on the measuring machine)
  led to the commits after the first README: `untaggedTerms` `"pass"` once
  bee-san/hoshidicts#58 merged; the noun-first order, the -ati rule and the
  preferred readings; the subjunctive agreement; the `ce la`, `ci siamo`,
  `stessi`, dire and trarre changes; the article `i`; lookup rows for each
  group of preferred readings, test rows for the strong verbs and the
  minimum stems, an untagged tiny dictionary with form-of rows; and a 700-row
  sample, so that `tests/` and `sources/` stay under 1 MB (993,080 bytes).

## Known gaps

- **Ties the orders and the preferred readings do not settle.** Where a
  word's own rows name two lemmas, both reached in one step, the lookup
  shows the one it found first (9.6). Outside the top 10,000 of it_50k no
  preferred reading applies, and these remain (Measurements 2 and 4):
  - feminine plural adjectives whose feminine singular is also a noun show
    the noun: about 90 words of ranks 10,001-50,000 (22 of a random 40 of
    the 165 in the class: `finanziarie`, `mistiche`, `chimiche`, `italiane`,
    `storiche`), and in the five texts about 160 of 455 lookups (`figure
    tratte dalla vita reale`, `statue classiche`). The opposite order is
    worse (Rule provenance: 23 against 21 in the top 10,000);
  - -i plurals whose rows name an -e noun and an -o adjective show the noun
    (`testi` → teste, `scuri` → scure, `fari` → fare: 7 of the 33 such words
    of ranks 10,001-50,000; 1 of 20 in the texts);
  - -ati and -iti words whose rows name only the -rsi imperative show it
    before the rules' participle, because the dictionary's own reading
    outranks a rule's (`eliminati`, `aiutati`, `iniettati`: 14 of a random
    40 of the 81 in ranks 10,001-50,000; in the texts 10 of 20 of 67
    lookups, `vetri appannati`, `sassi cacciati`). Where the rows name both,
    the -ati rule puts the participle first, so `sfogati` and `affrettati`,
    which are usually imperatives, now show the participle;
  - other ties: an -are and an -ere reading (`stringo` → stringare);
    words used both ways keep the noun (46 of the top 10,000: `ricordi`,
    `lavori`, `aspetti`, `scherzi`); `odi` shows udire; `messi` shows messo
    ("messenger"), since its rows name messo and messe, not mettere.

  A frequency dictionary would order all of these, since the lookup compares
  frequencies before the order of discovery. `lookup.confirmedOrder:
  "references"`, queued for the framework (hachi-lang-lead's answer of
  2026-11-03T18:46Z to hachi-lang-ru), would let the dictionary's bank
  order decide instead; for the feminine plurals of ranks 10,001-50,000 that
  order shows the adjective for 96 of the 165 words, but also
  `caratteristiche` → caratteristico and `curve` → curvo.
- **Ties decided by a processor variant.** The variants of a word are looked
  up in code point order, and a candidate keeps the place it was first found
  in: `starò` shows starare (found first through `staro`, starare's
  present). A capitalised word as written needs no processor step, so a
  capital letter's own entry comes before the decapitalised word at the
  start of a sentence: `E`, `A`, `I` and `O` show the letters, 1,120 of the
  233,208 Gutenberg lookups (0.48%). This is the same in every Latin-script
  pack; hachi-lang-lead's answer of 2026-11-03T09:45Z to hachi-lang-de makes
  it a host follow-up (a request flag for the scan's first word).
- **`ti` + singular present subjunctive** gives no -rsi verb any more: about
  24 right readings of the 157 Tatoeba places are lost (`che tu ti senta`,
  `ti metta comodo`), and 513 UniMorph rows of -ere and -ire verbs
  (Measurements 3). The io present subjunctive after mi was left out
  before, for the same reason: these are the two `mi` rows of `gaps.tsv`
  (`mi prolunghi`, `mi invigorisca`).
- **The other 9 rows of `gaps.tsv`** are feminine nouns whose kaikki lemma is
  the masculine (`tabaccaia` → tabaccaio, `boera`, `uruguaiana`,
  `cravattaia`, `emesena`, `conservativa`, `autoreclusa`, `aracnofoba`,
  `feltrina`). The feminine rule writes `adj`, and the corpus rows ask for
  `n`; with `"pass"` a lookup still finds each of them through that rule,
  since an untagged row passes any condition.
- An impersonal or passive si is read as the -rsi verb (`si vedeva un
  caminetto` → vedersi, `si studia francese` → studiarsi, `si poteva` →
  potersi, 128 Tatoeba places for potere and dovere), an object pronoun
  before an imperfect subjunctive agrees with io (`mi dicessi` → dirsi), and
  an object ci is read into a -rci verb (`ci aiuterebbe` → aiutarci): 6 of
  60 Tatoeba and 10 of 60 Gutenberg -rsi readings in the samples.
- Clitic climbing is read with stare + gerund and dovere, potere and volere +
  infinitive, not with sapere (`vi sapete allacciare` → sapersi), andare or
  venire, nor with the past historic of a modal (`si dovè accorgere` →
  doversi); with ci it gives only the -rsi verb (`ci stiamo lavorando` →
  lavorarsi, `ci sta mettendo` → starci).
- `ce ne` before a noi form keeps the reflexive pair (`ce ne andiamo` →
  andarsene); `ci anderemo` gives andarsi and `ci picchiamo` piccarsi, both
  ties of equal trace length.
- `mi comperò` gives comparso: the whole-word reading of `comperò` covers the
  word alone, not after a pronoun.
- **Text without accents.** OpenSubtitles often leaves the final accent out
  (it_50k: `perche` 489,341, `gia` 205,703, `cosi` 369,960, `fara` 25,996
  beside `farà` 25,890). With the defaults, `perche` shows perca, `cosi`
  coso, `gia` giara and `fara` faro. The optional `addFinalAccent` processor
  (setting `missingAccents`) adds the accented reading, but after any reading
  of the word as written, since a variant without a processor step ranks
  first: `puo` → può and `piu` → più come first, `perche` → perché and `fara`
  → fare second, `citta` → città and `gia` → già third.
- No rule reads diminutives, augmentatives or pejoratives (-ino, -etto, -one,
  -accio): `camerina` is read as camerino. wty-it-en's form-of rows name the
  common ones (`casetta` → casa).
- Old spellings are read only where a rule makes them (`minaccie`, `dovè`,
  `anderò`, `varii`).
- Proper names that `decapitalize` lowers can be read as words (`Póntina` →
  pontare), and Roman numerals as words (`VI.` → Vi, `VII.` → via).
- Removing `dell'` or `un'` from `removeElision` changes no test, because
  Yomitan's `removeApostrophedWords` makes the same variants.
- No test can observe `traceSelection` (`lookup.tsv` checks a result's
  expression and matched text, not its trace), so it is set as the contract
  recommends.
