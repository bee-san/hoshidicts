# Albanian (`sq`)

Deinflection rules, text processors and scanning settings for Albanian
(standard, Tosk-based spelling). Lookups start at the hovered word and try
only prefixes that end at a word boundary (`word` / `word` / `spaces`,
docs/languages.md 14.1). An apostrophe or hyphen ends a word, so *ministri*
in *ish-ministri* and *kam* in *s’kam* can be looked up, and the rules read
the contractions *s’* and *ç’* (`s’kam` → `kam`). The analytic tenses are
read from their first word, as the dictionary's own form-of rows read them
(`kam punuar`, `do të shkoj`, `u largua`, `duke ecur` → `punoj`, `shkoj`,
`largohem`, `eci`), and so is an adjective with its article (`e madhe`,
`më i mirë` → `madh`, `mirë`).

## Rule provenance

`transforms.json` has 151 transforms and 2,286 rules (2,562 with the
circumfix cross products expanded): 2,159 `own`, 127 `both` and no
`yomitan` rule. `sources/build-final.mjs` writes it: `sources/own.json` with
every Yomitan rule left out and the own rules that make a Yomitan rule's
deinflection marked `both`.

- **`both`**: an own rule of the same type in the transform of the same
  form that replaces the same ending with the same text, or a longer ending
  with a common part in front (Yomitan's aorist `va` → `j` is the own `ova` →
  `oj`, `eva` → `ej`, `iva` → `ij`, … on the stems Yomitan's rule reaches),
  and only when the own rule is in the first commit of this pack, `sq: own
  rules` (History below). `LATER` in `build-final.mjs` keeps 44 rules `own`
  that Yomitan also has, because they were written after its file was read:
  `oi` → `ua`, the aorist `ye` → `ej`, 13 mediopassive endings to `hem`, the
  definite of the 21 listed masculine nouns in -ë (`djali` → `djalë`), and
  the 7 noun and present rules at longer endings of audit-hachi-lang-sq-b
  (`ta` → `të`, `ën` → `ë`, `uan` → `uaj`, …) and `uni` → `un` of
  audit-hachi-lang-sq-d. Without it 171 rules would be
  `both` and 91 Yomitan rules paired. 88 of
  Yomitan's 94 rules have such own rules (`sources/yomitan-provenance.tsv`
  lists them). The other six are the nominalization (`im` → `oj`, `im` →
  `ej`, `je` → ``) and three whose only own counterparts came later:
  `definite` `i` → `ë`, `oi` → `ua` and the aorist `ye` → `ej`.

- **Lists and homographs** (after audit-hachi-lang-sq): the masculine
  nouns in -ë that drop it before -i, -in and -it are a list of 21 (djalë,
  kalë, lumë, ujë, gjumë, burrë, babë, mamë, …), since most nouns in -ë are
  feminine; `zë` has `zëri`; `dita`, `ditën` and `ditët` read first as
  `ditë` (day), and wty-sq-en's rows still give the aorist of `di` second;
  `më` reads adverbs only from a list of gradable ones and those in -isht
  (`më vonë` → `vonë`, `më saktësisht` → `saktësisht`), so `deri më sot`
  (until today) is not a comparative.
- **Longer endings** (after audit-hachi-lang-sq-b): the deinflector tries
  the longest ending first, and the file order decides only between rules
  at one ending (docs/languages.md 7.4). So where wty-sq-en names a noun and
  a verb for a word and the verb's ending is longer, the verb came first:
  `jeta` → `jes` (aorist `-ta`) before `jetë` (life, `-a`), `tokën` → `tok`
  (`-ën`) before `tokë`, `lidhja` → `lidh` (imperfect `-ja`) before
  `lidhje`. Noun rules at those endings make the same deinflections as the
  shorter ones (`ta` → `të`, `ka` → `kë`, `ja` → `je`, `ja` → `jë` after a
  vowel but o, `jta` → `jtë`, `ën` → `ë`, `jtën` → `jtë`, `ke` → `kë`), so
  `jeta`, `veta`, `tokën`, `lidhja`, `lindja`, `lëvizja`, `pika`, `daja`
  and `maja` show the noun. vij's forms `vini`, `vija`, `vije`, `vinte`,
  `vinim`, `vinit`, `vinin` come before vë's; `vija`, `bija`, `goditja`
  and `zgjidhja` are whole-word nouns, and `mundja` (I could), `ftoi` (he
  invited), `zotërinj` (gentlemen), `male` (mountains) and `të marrë` (that
  he take) are listed the other way; the non-active consonant stems read
  the mediopassive verb first (`ulet` → `ulem`, `dukej` → `dukem`), as the
  vowel stems did; and the present of the -uaj verbs at the aorist's ending
  (`ruan` → `ruaj`, present).
- **Stem constraints** keep the rules off the words they would misread:
  - every suffix rule needs a stem without a space, so the last word of a
    phrase is never read on its own (`e ka`, `të mira`);
  - minimum stems and final-letter classes: `hej` is not `hell`, `pra` is
    not `pre`, `lart` is not `lar`, `mira`, `mia`, `mija` and `miri` are
    not `mi` (`-ri` after i needs three letters before it, `-ja` comes after
    a vowel other than i, so `hija` is `hije`), `urdhërin` is not `urdhë`,
    `VI` is not `vij`, `vetmi` is not the noun `vetëm` (it is the definite
    adjective, `i vetmi`), `hyjni` is not `hyj` (a verb stem before a consonant ending
    does not end in j; the non-active endings have twin rules for the
    digraphs gj and nj, so `përgjigjet` → `përgjigjem`);
  - the one-syllable verbs (jam, kam, ha, pi, di, rri, fle, lë, vë, zë,
    nxë, bie, dua, vete) have whole-word forms, so `dalësh` is not da- +
    `lësh`;
  - after an auxiliary only the participle rules continue (`për të marrë`
    → `marr`, not the adjective `i marrë`), and after `të` the oblique
    definite adjective is read only in -s and -it after `e`, `së` and `i`,
    and in -it only for adjectives in -r, since after `të` these endings are
    genitive nouns (`të fizikës`, `të lumit`); the feminine oblique in -s is
    read only after `së` (`e matematikës` is a genitive noun);
  - the mediopassive lemma of the non-active aorist, perfect and gerund is
    read only after `u` and the non-active auxiliaries (circumfix rules:
    `u largova`, `është ulur`, `duke u larë`), not after `ka` (`ka ardhur`
    is not `ardhem`).
